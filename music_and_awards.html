Musical Maestros & Global Honors

Music has never served merely as incidental accompaniment in Tamil cinema; it is the philosophical heartbeat and narrative soul of the medium. From rural operettas to contemporary electronic sound design, the songs and instrumental background scores crafted in Chennai recording studios have transcended linguistic frontiers to achieve global renown.

In the 1960s, the duo M. S. Viswanathan & T. K. Ramamoorthy (lovingly titled Mellisai Mannargal) introduced sophisticated acoustic polyphony, blending waltzes, Latin rhythms, and classical ragas with deep emotional intimacy. In 1976, Ilaiyaraaja initiated an unprecedented golden revolution by infusing indigenous folk melodies with Western Baroque counterpoint and full symphonic orchestration. Composing over a thousand motion picture scores and symphonies recorded with the Royal Philharmonic Orchestra in London, his scoring techniques established the benchmark for cinematic background composition.

The dawn of the 1990s heralded A. R. Rahman, whose revolutionary sound engineering in Roja (1992) modernized Indian film music forever. Rahman’s visionary synthesis of electronic keyboards, Qawwali, reggae, and traditional Tamil percussion led him to earn two Academy Awards (Oscars), two Grammy Awards, a BAFTA, and a Golden Globe.
