Nayagan (1987) — The Neo-Realist Noir Triumph

Director: Mani Ratnam
Music Director: Ilaiyaraaja
Cinematography: P. C. Sreeram
Lead Star: Kamal Haasan (as Velu Naicker)
Genre: Crime / Drama / Neo-Noir
Accolades: TIME All-Time 100 Best Films • 3 National Awards

The year 1987 marked an indelible watershed moment in world cinema with the premiere of Nayagan. Inspired broadly by the life of Bombay underworld patriarch Varadarajan Mudaliar and Mario Puzo’s The Godfather, Mani Ratnam crafted an intensely authentic regional portrait of migration, survival, and moral ambiguity in the slums of Dharavi.

Kamal Haasan delivered what critics globally cite as one of cinema’s most transformative performances. Aging over three decades on screen from an enraged young vigilante to an elderly patriarch bearing the physical burdens of his past, Haasan earned the prestigious National Film Award for Best Actor.

Technically, cinematographer P. C. Sreeram disrupted prevailing Indian studio conventions by employing naturalistic single-source lighting, rain reflections, and tight close-ups. In 2005, TIME Magazine enshrined Nayagan in its elite “All-Time 100 Best Films” list alongside works by Akira Kurosawa, Stanley Kubrick, and Francis Ford Coppola.

Complementing the arresting visuals was Ilaiyaraaja’s operatic background score, particularly the haunting anthem “Thenpandi Cheemayile,” which elevated a gritty underworld chronicle into a universal Greek tragedy exploring the duality of virtue and sin.
